PROPOSED TEXT FOR THE PSM SECTION

We assess covariate balance using standardized mean differences (SMDs), rather
than relying on null-hypothesis significance tests. Table A6 reports treated
and control means, SMDs before and after matching, and variance ratios for
continuous covariates. Across the covariates and categorical levels used in
the propensity-score model, matching reduces the maximum absolute SMD from
0.35 before matching to 0.06 after matching; no matched-on row exceeds the
conventional absolute-SMD threshold of 0.10 after matching. Figure A2 provides
the corresponding Love plot. These results indicate substantial improvement
in balance on observed characteristics, but they do not establish balance on
unobserved determinants of venture-debt receipt.

Figure A1 displays the estimated propensity-score distributions before and
after matching. All 1,410 treated firms lie within the empirical overlap
interval [0.068, 0.691]. Of the 5,128 eligible controls, 5,081 lie within this
interval and 47 lie outside it; the final matched sample contains 1,410 treated
firms and 1,410 controls. The post-matching distributions show improved common
support, although perfect density overlap is neither required nor observed.
Accordingly, Figure A1 is evidence about overlap in estimated propensity
scores, not proof that matching eliminates endogeneity or unobserved selection.

The actual/pseudo reference-year row and prior-patent row in Table A6 are
reported as additional diagnostics. They were not included in the rebuilt
historical propensity-score specification and therefore are labeled
"Matched on = No." Their post-match mean SMDs are small (0.011 and 0.012 in
absolute value), but the variance ratio for reference year remains 3.74. This
indicates that the matched groups have similar average reference years but
different dispersion in timing. It should be disclosed rather than described
as complete calendar-time balance, and it motivates specifications with year
fixed effects and the separate staggered-DiD analysis.


REVISED NOTE FOR TABLE A6

Table A6 reports covariate balance before and after 1:1 nearest-neighbor
propensity-score matching. Balance is evaluated primarily using standardized
mean differences (SMDs); absolute SMDs below 0.10 are used as a diagnostic
benchmark, not as a formal hypothesis test. Variance ratios are reported for
continuous variables. Categorical covariates are evaluated separately for
each level rather than by comparing arbitrary numeric category codes. The
reference-year and prior-patent rows are supplementary diagnostics and were
not included in the historical propensity-score model. The post-matching
sample includes 1,410 VD-backed firms and 1,410 matched controls. These
statistics assess balance on observed covariates only and do not resolve
selection on unobservables.


REVISED NOTE FOR FIGURE A1

Figure A1 plots the estimated propensity-score distributions for VD-backed
and control firms before and after matching. The post-matching panel shows
improved overlap and the final matched sample lies within the treated group's
empirical propensity-score range. The plot does not require identical kernel
densities and does not establish conditional independence or eliminate
unobserved selection.


NOTE FOR FIGURE A2 (LOVE PLOT)

Figure A2 plots the absolute standardized mean difference for each continuous
covariate and categorical level before and after matching. The vertical line
at 0.10 is a conventional diagnostic benchmark. All covariates and levels
used in the propensity-score model have post-matching absolute SMDs below
0.10. Reference year and prior patent are shown as supplementary diagnostics
and are identified as not having been included in the score model.
